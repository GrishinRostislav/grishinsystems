I very often see posts and comments saying that CCNA is an entry-level certification in networking. But after starting to prepare for the exam, I realized that this is not the case at all.

I have been working with networks for over 10 years. And, honestly, for most of the tasks I was well paid for, it was enough to know and understand the following things:

* Router replacement and configuration (WAN).
* Understanding what was connected where.
* Setting up DHCP, VPN, DDNS.
* Port forwarding for external connections for CCTV or Philips Dynalite smart lighting.

![Networking and CCNA](/images/networkWithCCNA.png)

In the automation and smart home industry, where I worked for a long time, very few people actually use different subnets or VLANs to separate traffic. Everyone just puts everything into one pile, as long as it works.

After I started studying for the CCNA, I finally REALLY understood why hidden problems constantly popped up in large-scale projects. Why the same Control4 or audio-video systems sometimes behaved unpredictably, signals to turn a device on or off were lost, something was always glitching and lagging, and most of the time it was solved simply by rebooting all the equipment.

Therefore, a deep understanding of networks allows companies to save precious time and money on service calls that could have been avoided back at the project implementation stage.
